## Why we ran this
Sales kept hearing "we need our own invoice layout" in late-stage deals, and Rohit had lost two deals where it was the stated reason. Meenal wanted to know whether template customisation was a real daily problem or a deal-time objection before it was sized.

## Method
Nine interviews with finance managers and billing admins at customers on the mid-market plan, 40 minutes each, over two weeks. Rohit sat in on three and took notes; I led all of them. I used the same five prompts each time: last time you sent an invoice, what did you change, who asked you to change it, what do you do when the layout is wrong. Priyanka answered feasibility questions in a separate thirty-minute call and did not join the interviews.

## What we heard
- Seven of nine edit a PDF after the invoice is generated, usually to add a PO number or change the footer. The edit is a daily chore for two of them and monthly for the rest.
- The stated need is "our logo and colours". The real friction is the PO number and a legal footer that differs by entity.
- Two customers had built a scripted workaround that adds the footer. Neither wanted to maintain it.
- Nobody asked for a drag-and-drop editor. When shown a sketch of one, four said they would not have time to configure it.

## Framing
The problem as sold was "custom templates". The problem as observed is "invoice needs fields it does not have: PO number, per-entity footer". Meenal and I agreed that sizing the latter is cheaper and testable. We wrote two hypotheses for the next step: (a) adding a PO number field and per-entity footer removes the PDF edit for most; (b) logo and colour alone does not.

## Next
Meenal took the field-level option into planning. I ran a short concept test with five of the nine in January (notes in the study folder): four said the footer solved the edit, one still needed a bespoke layout for a regulated entity. That customer is routed to professional services, not the product.
