## When to use this
Any study that needs current customers: interviews, task testing, diary studies. This page covers who to ask, how to ask, and what we keep. It does not cover usability testing of internal tools (see the internal tools page in PM).

## Steps
1. Write the research question and the participant profile before touching the customer list. "Daily reconciliation operator, at least six months on the product" is a profile; "some customers" is not.
2. Ask customer success for candidates, not for everyone. Imtiaz's team shares a sheet with account, role, tenure and last contact. Do not contact a customer who has an open escalation or a renewal inside 30 days.
3. Send the invitation from your own address, copying the CSM. Three lines: why, how long, what happens with notes. Offer two time slots in IST and one outside it.
4. Consent: send the consent text from Sneha's template one day before. Ask again verbally at the start. Sessions are not recorded unless the participant agrees in writing; most do not.
5. Do not collect account numbers, customer names of their end customers, or screenshots with live data. If a participant shares a screen, ask them to use a test tenant or blur before the call.
6. Thank-you: a gift voucher from the research budget, not a discount or credit. Credits create billing exceptions.

## What we keep
Anonymised notes, task outcomes and the recruitment sheet (without contact details after 90 days). Raw notes live in the study folder; this wiki holds the write-up.

## Lessons from the last three studies
- Response rate to the three-line invitation was 31 percent in 2025, against 12 percent for the older long-form email.
- Administrators are over-recruited because CSMs know them best. Ask explicitly for operators.
- A second observer is worth the scheduling effort; in the exception queue study they caught four wrong selections the facilitator missed.
