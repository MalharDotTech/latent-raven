We started with the assumption that finance operators needed a faster bulk accept action. Six observed sessions did not support that as the first change. Operators paused on mismatched currency and missing references; a faster accept would make the wrong action easier. Participants were existing customers who agreed to a moderated session. This is a small workflow sample, not an estimate of all users.

I changed the pilot scope to per-row reasons and saved filters. The success check is review time with no increase in incorrect acceptance. Click count by itself would reward skipping the check. The designer reviewed the task script and removed a prompt that told people where the filter was.

Sales requested scheduled exports for one account during the same milestone. We agreed a manual weekly report for that account through the pilot and wrote down who sends it. Finance ops backed the filter priority after reviewing repeat triage time. Engineering gave us the dependency estimate; product made the scope decision.

Follow-up: three returning operators reused saved filters. One could not find reset, so we sent the placement issue back to design. We kept export scheduling outside the next milestone until filter adoption stabilised. The September review retains that ordering and records the sales objection rather than deleting it from the roadmap notes.

Support completed the pilot enablement steps in a test account. Their first walkthrough exposed an unclear rollback owner, now assigned to the recon rotation. I prepared the release note and checked it with support. Account permissions and backend retry design are documented by engineering on the implementation page.
